      How it decides

      • Was the "was" price ever the price? A genuine reference price is one the shop actually charged, for a reasonable period, immediately before the sale. One visit can't prove that, so each check is stored and the verdict firms up over days and weeks.
      • Does the sale ever end? An item that's been "on sale" at every check for a month is just at its normal price.
      • Is the clock real? Countdown timers whose deadline is computed from your own clock, or that restart between visits, are props.
      • Pressure tactics. "Only 3 left", "12 people are viewing this", "up to 70% off" and RRP-based discounts each add to the suspicion score. None alone proves a fake sale; together they usually do.

      Sale Check can only read what a page sends to a plain browser. Shops that draw prices with JavaScript or block automated visits will come back as "no price found". The command-line version can analyse a page you have saved from your browser.
